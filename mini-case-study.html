Mini case study: turning one offer into a clearer launch page.

This page documents the same fixed-scope process used for Launch Page Express: clarify the offer, write the page around one decision, add a useful lead magnet, and make the next step easy.

See the processView sample pages
Context

The constraint was useful: no brand, no audience, no ad budget.

The service needed a public page that could be shared in communities without fake proof or exaggerated claims. The goal was not to look like a large agency; it was to make one small offer understandable and easy to request.

Starting point

  • Offer: fixed-scope landing page + lead magnet.
  • Audience: freelancers, creators, coaches, and consultants.
  • Price: USD 49 experiment package.
  • Boundary: static HTML/CSS, no hosting or revenue promises.

The process in four decisions.

1 · Offer

Package the smallest useful outcome

Instead of selling “web design,” the offer became one focused page, concise copy, and a short checklist or worksheet.

2 · Page

Write around one buying decision

The page answers who it is for, what is included, what is not included, how the process works, and what to send first.

3 · Proof

Use public samples instead of fake testimonials

The portfolio includes three fictional demo pages, a sample lead magnet, and worksheets visitors can inspect without signing up.

4 · Friction

Give prospects a ready-to-send request

The request checklist asks for six details so a buyer can start without a call, long form, or vague “tell me more” email.

What changed

The page now sells a decision, not a menu of services.

A visitor can see the examples, understand the scope, use a free resource, and send a clear request from one path. That makes the offer easier to share in value-first comments because the useful assets stand on their own.

Request a page like this

Use this as a template for your own service page.

If you are selling a small service, write the page in this order: audience, outcome, package boundaries, proof, process, FAQ, and request checklist. Leave out anything that makes the buyer wonder what happens next.
